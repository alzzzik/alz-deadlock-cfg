# Find Deadlock's last FPS without losing looks

Only one new setting has a real chance of raising fight FPS on your Ryzen 7 5700X: **`r_citadel_disable_particle_lights`**. Valve added it in build 6779 (2026-10-13). It is a `release` convar, so you can switch it on and off in the console during a fight before you commit it to `gameinfo.gi` ([GameTracking 0c38f9d](https://github.com/SteamDatabase/GameTracking-Deadlock/commit/0c38f9d)). Almost everything else that `gameinfo.gi` can still do for CPU load is either already in your config, already Valve's default, ignored online, or something that hides information. The research found **no measured per-convar FPS numbers** for any Deadlock setting since City Never Sleeps, so every gain below is an educated guess you have to confirm in your own A/B tests. The bigger change is in `video.txt`. Most of liv's "low" choices there (no anti-aliasing, mip bias 4, shader quality 0, no soft particles, no area lights) only lighten the GPU, and your RTX 5070 is mostly idle in fights. Those cuts cost picture quality and give back roughly zero fight FPS. So the best plan for your PC is to cut the few remaining CPU costs and put the spare GPU capacity back into how the game looks. The new notes also overturn several earlier recommendations. **`r_render_portals`, `r_particle_shadows`, `r_env_map_uses_height_map` and `r_reduce_flash` are dead keys.** The round 3 "camera-turn stutter" evidence was retracted by its author. And `thread_pool_option 2`, `r_update_particles_on_render_only_frames true` and the `snd_steamaudio_num_threads` reasoning don't do what the config claims. Every change below stays inside the `ConVars` block or `video.txt`, outside the seven guarded `gameinfo.gi` sections that matchmaking checks ([GameTracking 4e0f960](https://github.com/SteamDatabase/GameTracking-Deadlock/commit/4e0f960)).

The previous report and the alz-pc files were written against build ~6769 with a convar catalog that predates the October builds. Re-checking against Valve's build-6785 convar dump and the latest community repos shows several recommendations were wrong or rest on withdrawn evidence. The table lists each one and what to do now.

Texture filtering isn't in the menu, so the `gameinfo.gi` value applies. Anisotropic filtering costs about 1-2% on modern GPUs when the GPU is the limit and roughly nothing when the CPU is ([Guru3D forum](https://forums.guru3d.com/threads/question-about-4x-16x-anisotropic-texture-filtering-and-performance.427505/page-2)). Bilinear blurs floors and walls at an angle and adds visible mip bands. **Keep liv's 16x (`5`).** It is compylock's one deliberate quality choice compared with every other pack.

## Particle lights are the only new CPU lever in gameinfo.gi

Valve's build 6769 locked about 40 lighting convars to protect the game's art direction. A developer relayed through Sqooky said Valve would "improve performance elsewhere before adding more restrictions" and that "some performance cvars would come soon" ([OptimizationLock #88](https://github.com/Sqooky/OptimizationLock/issues/88)). Four days later build 6779 shipped `r_citadel_disable_particle_lights` (default `false`, flags `clientdll release`), described as "Disable particle lights except when explicitly overriden in particle renderer ops" ([GameTracking 0c38f9d](https://github.com/SteamDatabase/GameTracking-Deadlock/commit/0c38f9d)). Sqooky's 3.5.2 config adopted it with the credit "Thank you Dohn VALVe for pushing and telling me about this command", and Kaizu and Sqooky's max-FPS config followed ([Sqooky gameinfo.gi](https://github.com/Sqooky/OptimizationLock/blob/main/Sqooky's%20.gi/gameinfo.gi)). Particle lights are the dynamic lights that ability effects spawn. Now that `lb_enable_dynamic_lights` can't be set from `gameinfo.gi`, they are the light load that grows with fight size. Turning them off saves GPU shading and some per-frame CPU light setup. **Nobody has published an FPS number yet**, and the DMM catalog predates the convar. The visual cost is an important difference from the blocked lighting convars: the effects still draw, they just stop lighting up the floor and nearby heroes, and lights that particle authors set explicitly stay. That leaves ability effects looking a little flatter but hides nothing, which fits the "still looks good" goal.

The rest of the CPU block has little room left. The engine already enables every threading switch by default (`r_threaded_particles`, `cl_parallel_readpacketentities`, `phys_step_threaded`, `animgraph_parallel_postdataupdate` and so on), and Valve's own stock tail sets the parallel spatial-partition lines ([Valve convar dump](https://github.com/SteamDatabase/GameTracking-Deadlock/blob/master/DumpSource2/convars.txt); [Valve stock gameinfo.gi](https://github.com/SteamDatabase/GameTracking-Deadlock/blob/master/game/citadel/gameinfo.gi)). Most of the "FPS" lines in Sqooky 3.5.2's new block are `gamedll`-only (`ai_think_interval*`, `nav_obstruction_async_update`, `minimap_update_rate_hz`, `citadel_use_pvs_for_players`) or `replicated` (`skeleton_instance_lod_optimization`). Online, the server's value wins, so they only look good when tested in bot matches. **Run every CPU A/B in a real match**, not in the hideout or Sandbox. There the local server applies those values and makes the numbers look better than they are online. The forum picture hasn't changed either: fight hitching grows with team-fight size even on a 9800X3D at about 15% GPU load, with one core pinned ([9800X3D teamfight hitching thread](https://forums.playdeadlock.com/threads/frametime-stutter-hitching-that-scales-with-teamfight-size-%E2%80%94-9800x3d-rtx-4070-high-avg-fps.150957/)). Config lines trim that load; they don't remove it.

The particle fallback block (`cl_particle_sim_fallback_threshold_ms 1`, `_base_multiplier 100`, `cl_particle_fallback_base 5`, `_multiplier 10`) is the right kind of lever and should stay. Valve's particle schema shows that every effect has an artist-made "fallback replacement definition" (`m_hFallback`), and that field is separate from culling ([CParticleSystemDefinition.h](https://github.com/SteamDatabase/GameTracking-Deadlock/blob/master/DumpSource2/schemas/particles/CParticleSystemDefinition.h)). So under load the game swaps in Valve-made cheaper versions of effects rather than hiding them. Effects with no fallback are untouched. The earlier suspicion that the fallbacks removed the headshot particle was wrong: Sqooky traced that to the flashing-effects setting ([OptimizationLock #63](https://github.com/Sqooky/OptimizationLock/issues/63)). If one ability ever looks wrong, ghempy53's milder 2 / 20 / 2 / 5 is the fallback setting to try ([ghempy53 CHANGES.txt](https://github.com/ghempy53/deadlock-config/blob/main/docs/CHANGES.txt)).

Three other CPU lines deserve a single-variable test. `r_particle_model_per_thread_count` defaults to 32 and you run 64. If it is the batch size per particle-model job, which is the only reading the name supports, 64 halves the number of jobs your 16 threads can share in a fight. Nobody has measured it either way, and ghempy53 dropped it during a crash bisect ([ghempy53 CHANGES.txt](https://github.com/ghempy53/deadlock-config/blob/main/docs/CHANGES.txt)). Valve's 32 is the safer starting point. `r_particle_skip_postsim true` (a "testvar" with no description) and `cl_simulate_dormant_entities false` have no evidence at all and stay as they are unless something looks off. `citadel_player_model_force_highest_lod false` (build 6769, `release`) was adopted by Sqooky max-FPS, Kaizu and every OptiLock preset ([GameTracking 8f572f6](https://github.com/SteamDatabase/GameTracking-Deadlock/commit/8f572f6)). It only affects your own hero, which sits right in front of the camera, so the CPU-side note is right that the gain is negligible. Try it live only for curiosity.

`snd_mixahead 0.05` has no CPU evidence behind it; Sqooky's "shouldn't be perceivable" comment is the only justification ([Sqooky gameinfo.gi](https://github.com/Sqooky/OptimizationLock/blob/main/Sqooky's%20.gi/gameinfo.gi)). It does have a gameplay cost: up to 50 ms of extra audio buffering, which delays footstep and ability sounds against the picture. It is also an `archive` convar, so a saved value can override it ([Valve convar dump](https://github.com/SteamDatabase/GameTracking-Deadlock/blob/master/DumpSource2/convars.txt)). Remove it. The parse also found two silent conflicts. `sc_layer_batch_threshold_fullsort` is set to "120" by liv and then to "20" by Valve's stock tail further down. `snd_ui_positional` goes "false" then "1". The adamescj fork reports that "only the *last* line counts", which would make liv's values dead ([adamescj/OptimizationLock](https://github.com/adamescj/OptimizationLock)). Deleting liv's copies makes the file say what actually happens.

## Two free GPU-side gains in gameinfo.gi, and four lighting lines that cost looks

On the GPU side, only two settings that can still be set from `gameinfo.gi` are both real and invisible. **`csm_max_visible_dist 0`** (default 7500, `cheat`, which still applies from `gameinfo.gi` at startup) is used by every compared pack except Kaizu and OptiLock FPS, including adamescj's visuals-first Light config. Boot is credited with "csm cvars which had a notable performance improvement" ([OptimizationLock README](https://github.com/Sqooky/OptimizationLock); [adamescj Light](https://github.com/adamescj/OptimizationLock/tree/main/adamescj's%20light%20config)). With `r_rendersun 0` and zero cascades already set, it changes nothing you can see. It is the cleanest way to make sure the sun-shadow pass is really off, if the engine still renders it, which nobody has checked. Add `csm_cascade0..3_override_dist 0` alongside it. The second is **`volume_fog_width/height/depth` at the engine minimum, 64/64/48**, also in `gameinfo.gi`, as a backup in case `video.txt` doesn't apply these non-menu keys. ghempy53 confirms that `volume_fog_*` still works from `gameinfo.gi` ([ghempy53 BLOCKED-CONVARS.md](https://github.com/ghempy53/deadlock-config/blob/main/docs/BLOCKED-CONVARS.md)). Liv's 16/16/16 already clamped to that minimum, so the look doesn't change. Even together these are worth low single digits in quiet, GPU-bound moments and **about zero in CPU-bound fights**.

Everything else other packs use in this area either hides things or costs FPS. `fog_enable false` is in every other pack, but it removes compylock's atmosphere and DMM classes it as changing how far you can see ([DMM perf catalog](https://github.com/deadlock-mod-manager/deadlock-mod-manager/tree/main/packages/perf-catalog)). Skip it if you want compylock's look. `sc_cache_envmap_lpv_lookup false` and `lb_barnlight_shadow_use_precomputed_vis 0` turn off a cache and a precomputed-visibility optimization, so they most likely *add* CPU work. `r_particle_mixed_resolution_viewstart 800` draws more particles at full resolution. `sc_max_framebuffer_copies_per_layer 0` probably breaks screen-sampling effects such as refraction and cloak shimmer. `mat_colorcorrection 0` and `sc_hdr_enabled_override 0` make the game grey or clip highlights ([DeadTune](https://github.com/simulieren/deadtune); [DMM perf catalog](https://github.com/deadlock-mod-manager/deadlock-mod-manager/tree/main/packages/perf-catalog)). OptiLock 5.7's new ~40-line "TEST" block is mostly dead or restates defaults. Its live parts are aggressive LOD and clutter cuts (`sc_screen_size_lod_scale_override` down to 0.01), so don't copy it ([OptiLock commits](https://github.com/dacooderr/OptiLock/commits/main)).

Round 2's lighting lines deserve a second look, because with the sun, SSAO and dynamic shadows all off, **baked lightmaps now do almost all of the scene's shading**. Three lines degrade exactly that: `r_lightmap_size 2048` (default 65536; "lower values make baked lighting blurrier"), `r_lightmap_size_directional_irradiance 0` and `r_directional_lightmaps false` ("baked lighting looks flatter") ([DMM perf catalog](https://github.com/deadlock-mod-manager/deadlock-mod-manager/tree/main/packages/perf-catalog); [ghempy53 UPSTREAM-DIFF](https://github.com/ghempy53/deadlock-config/blob/main/docs/research/UPSTREAM-DIFF-2026-10-10.md)). Their cost is VRAM and texture bandwidth, not shading time, so reverting them should be close to free on a 12 GB card. Nobody has measured that, so check it in the same spot with frame-time telemetry on. A fourth line, `lb_precomputed_shadowmap_enable false`, is what ghempy53 grouped under "map becomes unreadably bright or dark". Your stationary lights still render because Valve blocked their off switch, and shadow casting is off, so this line could also let light leak through walls. Two more round-2 lines have no FPS reason at all: `volume_fog_enable_jitter false` (jitter is what hides banding in minimum-resolution fog) and `lb_cubemap_normalization_roughness_begin 0.01` (a shader constant). Remove both. `lb_csm_override_staticgeo_cascades_value "true"` is a parse error ("true is not a number") and does nothing. Keep `r_morphing_enabled false`: it is one of the few render lines that trims fight-time skinning work, and morph targets are barely visible from a third-person camera. Keep `r_distancefield_enable 0` too, but revert it first if any lighting looks wrong. The visuals-first Light config keeps it at 1 ([adamescj Light](https://github.com/adamescj/OptimizationLock/tree/main/adamescj's%20light%20config)).

## video.txt: liv's GPU cuts buy almost nothing on a 5070

`video.txt` sits above `gameinfo.gi` for every menu-owned setting, and most of liv's choices there are GPU-only. Valve's own help text for `mat_viewportscale` says it reduces "GPU impact on CPU profiling", and the menu tooltips rate anti-aliasing, bloom and area lights as "small" costs ([GT convars.txt](https://github.com/SteamTracking/GameTracking-Deadlock/blob/4e0f960a9d175b18757ac607c04e70f15dabe7e3/DumpSource2/convars.txt); [GT citadel_main_english.txt](https://github.com/SteamTracking/GameTracking-Deadlock/blob/4e0f960a9d175b18757ac607c04e70f15dabe7e3/game/citadel/resource/localization/citadel_main/citadel_main_english.txt)). Valve's Medium, High and Ultra presets all ship DLSS, `shaderquality 1` and mip bias 0-1. Only Low uses Stretch with no anti-aliasing ([GT video_defaults_1.txt](https://github.com/SteamTracking/GameTracking-Deadlock/blob/4e0f960a9d175b18757ac607c04e70f15dabe7e3/game/citadel/pak01_dir/cfg/video_defaults_1.txt)). Upscaling can't raise fight FPS on a CPU-bound PC, so use the spare GPU capacity for image quality instead. Set **DLSS at 1x (DLAA)** with the Transformer model, which NVIDIA describes as a "minor performance tradeoff" on RTX 50 ([HotHardware](https://hothardware.com/news/nvidia-dlss-45-benchmarked)). Set **texture quality to High (mip bias 0)**, because mip bias costs VRAM and streaming, not shader time, and compylock's own comment says to lower it if you have VRAM ([7liv/compylock](https://github.com/7liv/compylock)). Also turn on `shaderquality 1`, soft-particle feathering and area lights, which Valve's tooltip says render beam effects "correctly". The video-layer note estimates a 0-3% fight-FPS change for the whole set. That is an inference, so verify it. If FPS drops, revert in this order: DLAA to FXAA, then `shaderquality` back to 0, then feathering off.

Keep the `video.txt` settings that do cut CPU work: shadow quality 0, `csm_max_num_cascades_override 0`, `lb_enable_shadow_casting false` (fewer shadow passes means fewer draw calls), the particle fallbacks and fog quality 0. Keep Reflex at On (1); Boost only helps when the GPU is the limit. Keep `cpu_level 2` and `gpu_level 3`. Particle systems carry a "minimum CPU level" and "minimum GPU level", so lower values can drop whole effects ([CParticleSystemDefinition.h](https://github.com/SteamTracking/GameTracking-Deadlock/blob/4e0f960a9d175b18757ac607c04e70f15dabe7e3/DumpSource2/schemas/particles/CParticleSystemDefinition.h)). Change liv's `mem_level 0` and `gpu_mem_level 2` to what autodetect would pick for 32 GB of RAM and 12 GB of VRAM, **2 and 3** ([GT moddefaults.txt](https://github.com/SteamTracking/GameTracking-Deadlock/blob/4e0f960a9d175b18757ac607c04e70f15dabe7e3/game/citadel/pak01_dir/cfg/moddefaults.txt)). `mem_level 0` is the "under 4 GB" class, which only makes sense on a much weaker PC.

One open question could still give a real CPU gain: whether `video.txt` applies a convar that `gameinfo.gi` is barred from setting. The loader already sets `developmentonly` menu convars and uses a generic `setting.%s` mapping, and Valve's own presets carry stale keys without errors, so it might ([GT client_strings.txt](https://github.com/SteamTracking/GameTracking-Deadlock/blob/4e0f960a9d175b18757ac607c04e70f15dabe7e3/game/citadel/bin/win64/client_strings.txt)). That isn't proven. The only blocked key worth trying is **`r_particle_max_detail_level 2`**. Particle child systems are tagged Low/Medium/High/Ultra, so 2 drops only the Ultra-tagged children, which saves simulation work without hiding abilities. Avoid 0, which drops everything tagged Medium or higher ([ParticleChildrenInfo_t.h](https://github.com/SteamTracking/GameTracking-Deadlock/blob/4e0f960a9d175b18757ac607c04e70f15dabe7e3/DumpSource2/schemas/particles/ParticleChildrenInfo_t.h)).

The visual trade-off to flip is accessibility, not performance. `r_light_sensitivity_mode true` ("Reduce flashing effects") has no FPS effect, and Sqooky traces the missing white headshot particle to it ([OptimizationLock #63](https://github.com/Sqooky/OptimizationLock/issues/63)). Unless flashes bother you, turn it off to get that hit cue back. For the same reason, `cl_ragdoll_limit "0"` in `gameinfo.gi` still hides Doorman's ultimate indicator ([OptimizationLock README](https://github.com/Sqooky/OptimizationLock)). Use `-1`, which is also the only value that matters after the `r_render_portals` correction, since that key no longer exists.

## Conclusion

The research changes what "FPS config" means for your PC. A 5700X in a team fight has a fixed CPU budget, and `gameinfo.gi` can now trim only the parts of it Valve leaves open: particle lights, particle fallbacks and shadow passes. Every one of those is already in the config or arrives with this round. The rest of the 400-line file is mostly tidying, dead lines or Valve defaults written out again. The larger gain is in perceived quality. About half of liv's `video.txt` was tuned for a GPU-bound PC, and on a 5070 those cuts make the game look worse without making fights smoother. Bigger gains than any cfg line still sit outside the files (SMT off, confirmed 3200 MT/s RAM, shader cache), as the alz-pc README already says ([SMT-off forum report](https://forums.playdeadlock.com/threads/hyper-threading-or-smt-off-give-more-stable-fps-and-better-gpu-utilization.91848/)).

The retraction of the round 3 evidence also changes how to test from here. No source in this space, this config included, has a frame-time capture behind it. The only evidence that should promote a line is your own A/B in a real match, using the same spot, one variable at a time, with `cl_hud_telemetry_frametime_show` or CapFrameX recording 1% lows. Valve has also said more performance convars are coming, so check every new convars.txt diff for `release`-flagged `r_citadel_*` or `cl_particle_*` additions. That is where the next real gain will come from.
