## Q1. Which ConVars reduce main- or render-thread CPU time without changing what is drawn? (ranked candidate list)

### Takeaway
The cfg has almost no zero-visual CPU headroom left. Valve already defaults every threading switch to its parallel path, and the stock tail sets the parallel and spatial-partition lines. What is left falls into three groups:
- **(a) Hygiene lines.** These remove self-inflicted costs or no-ops: `snd_boxverb_simd false` turns a SIMD path off, `snd_steamaudio_num_threads 6` and `thread_pool_option 2` do nothing useful, and two keys are duplicated. Each is zero risk with an expected gain of about 0 to small.
- **(b) Undocumented particle and voice scheduling switches.** About six of these could plausibly cut main-thread stalls without changing pixels. None is measured.
- **(c) One `release` latency/pacing switch** (`engine_low_latency_sleep_after_client_tick`) that can be A/B'd live in the console.

The strongest real CPU levers (particle lights, particle detail level, fallbacks) all change what is drawn, so they go in the "rejected because visible" list or in Q4.

### Inferences
- **Ranking logic.** Items 1-2 can't plausibly cost FPS, but they act on the audio thread, not the main or render thread. The gain is likely below measurement noise on 16 threads, so they are clean-ups, not FPS. Item 3 is the only one that can be A/B'd mid-fight without a restart. It targets frame pacing on 64-tick frames under Reflex, and its likeliest effect is on 1% lows or latency, not average FPS. Items 4-7 are the only remaining zero-visual levers on the particle and voice paths, which are known to scale with fight size. Each needs its own A/B because none is documented.
- **`r_particle_model_new8 false` is the oddest line in the cfg.** It forces a non-default particle-model path that Valve ships `true`. Removing it is low-risk and returns the cfg to Valve's own tested path.
- **The −40 FPS experimental bundle can't be split by reasoning alone, but the likeliest CPU suspect is the round-3 reset.** That reset removed `r_limit_particle_job_duration`, `r_particle_max_size_cull 600`, `sc_*_threshold 256` and the texture-budget lines, and their only "harm" evidence is now invalidated. The bundle also held GPU-side lines that could cost more: `lb_precomputed_shadowmap_enable false`, `lb_dynamic_shadow_resolution false`, `r_directional_lightmaps false`, `csm_res_override_*`. Keep the round-3 lines as they are in stable/alz.
- `r_particle_skip_postsim true` may be skipping the parallel post-simulate pass for model particles. That pass is likely where `r_particle_model_per_thread_count` applies. If model-based effects (debris, models spawned by abilities) ever look frozen or misplaced, this line is the first suspect. Removing it would also make item 5 matter.

### Gaps
- No frame-time measurement exists for any line in this table on any hardware. Steam Community and ultimatemove.eu could not be reached from this session (DNS failure), and searches found no 2026 Reddit A/B posts.
- The meanings of `r_particle_model_new8`, `r_late_particle_job_sync`, `update_voices_low_priority` and `r_particle_skip_postsim` are inferred from names and string tables, not from Valve documentation.
- Whether the VMix boxverb or diffusor processors are in Deadlock's active mix graph (`snd_soundmixer "Default_Mix"`) is unverified, so items 1 and 8 may be no-ops.

## Q2. Which "CPU" lines in community configs are placebo (stock value, removed, blocked, server-only, bot-match only)?

### Takeaway
The alz cfg has 98 ConVar entries that do nothing. 19 are missing from the 6821 dump, 5 are blocked (`gameinfo_cannot_override`), 11 are server-only, 22 are `replicated`, and 41 restate Valve's default. Seven keys are duplicated, two of them with conflicting values. OptiLock 6.1's new "optimizations" block is mostly placebo too: server and replicated lines, defaults, and names that don't exist. `thread_pool_option` is a no-op on the 5700X.

## Q3. Is anything new in builds 6801-6821 relevant?

### Takeaway
Only one ConVar change happened in 6801-6821, and it matters: **build 6812 (2026-10-21) removed `gameinfo_cannot_override` from `r_particle_max_detail_level`** and gave it min 0 / max 3. Stock `gameinfo.gi` is byte-identical from 6801 to 6821 (the `PGIVersion "48C04ECA…"` in alz is current). No new threading or job ConVars appeared, and no defaults changed.

### Cited Findings
- Diff of `DumpSource2/convars.txt` 6801 → 6821: exactly one line, `r_particle_max_detail_level 3 (developmentonly defensive gameinfo_cannot_override)` → `r_particle_max_detail_level 3 (min: 0, max: 3, developmentonly defensive)`, help "The maximum detail level of particle to create" ([6812 commit 0abfe67, 2026-10-21](https://github.com/SteamDatabase/GameTracking-Deadlock/commit/0abfe676c64c7d24bd5340a46778e65a84be70ed)). The DMM catalog (convarBuild 6812) reflects this: "Highest particle detail level spawned; 0 spawns only the cheapest variants" ([catalog](https://github.com/deadlock-mod-manager/deadlock-mod-manager/blob/2d6f6bac1ff3fe1f398bfecbb139dd62cc10ca60/packages/perf-catalog/data/catalog.json)).
- Builds after 6801 (6802-6821, 2026-10-20 to 2026-10-26) touched `convars.txt` only in 6812. The rest were hero, ability and sound content (Nurse, Artist, Doorman doorway debug strings, walk-back item) and manifests ([GameTracking history](https://github.com/SteamDatabase/GameTracking-Deadlock/commits/master)). `game/citadel/gameinfo.gi` last changed in 6801 ([37f9aeb](https://github.com/SteamDatabase/GameTracking-Deadlock/commit/37f9aebdc62766aa13748f73309b9b8120549833)).
- No video-menu setting owns particle detail. Valve's `video_defaults_*.txt` and `popup_settings.xml` have no particle-detail convar ([popup_settings.xml](https://github.com/SteamDatabase/GameTracking-Deadlock/blob/d251cf9d9a796061c90f58724230b7e17b97121b/game/citadel/pak01_dir/panorama/layout/popups/popup_settings.xml)). So a gameinfo value will apply.
- OptiLock has carried `r_particle_max_detail_level "0"` since 2026-08-15 (git pickaxe on the FPS Config). Since 6812 it actually takes effect ([FPS Config](https://github.com/dacooderr/OptiLock/blob/8b78c0f9e09b767b60f7c90be473a9d3320b398e/OptiLock%20FPS%20Config%20(Recommended)/gameinfo.gi)).
- Steam patch notes and search results found no Valve performance notes for October 2026 ([liquipedia patches portal](https://liquipedia.net/deadlock/Portal:Patches); [deadlock.wiki patch notes](https://deadlock.wiki/Patch_notes)).

### Inferences
- `r_particle_max_detail_level` is now the biggest particle-CPU lever available from `gameinfo.gi`. It fails the zero-visual rule: values below 3 skip artist-tagged high-detail sub-effects. It belongs in "rejected because visible". If alz ever relaxes the rule, `2` is the only sane test value (it keeps base effects and drops the top tier), not OptiLock's `0`.
- Users of OptiLock's FPS/Potato presets got a silent particle-quality cut on 2026-10-21. Any "OptiLock got faster after the patch" report after that date is confounded by this.

### Gaps
- What detail level 3 vs 2 removes in concrete Deadlock effects is unknown. The compiled particle files are not in GameTracking, so no count of systems by detail tier was possible.

## Q4. Particle fallback ConVars: do they count as a visual cost?

### Takeaway
Yes, but conditionally. Fallbacks swap a particle system for its **Valve-authored cheaper replacement** only when particle simulation time per frame exceeds the threshold, and only for systems that define a fallback. At alz's 1 ms / ×100 / 5 / ×10, that happens in practically every team fight, so effects look somewhat simpler exactly when it matters. Under a strict "zero visible loss" rule, only Valve's defaults (6 ms / 5 / 0 / 0) qualify. The fallbacks are still the only particle lever that *targets* fight-time CPU and leaves quiet scenes untouched, and OptiLock moved to an even earlier trigger.

### Cited Findings
- Valve help: `cl_particle_sim_fallback_threshold_ms` (default 6): "Amount of simulation time that can elapse before new systems start falling back to cheaper versions". `cl_particle_sim_fallback_base_multiplier` (default 5): "How aggressive the switch to fallbacks will be…". `cl_particle_fallback_base` / `_multiplier` (default 0/0): "…falling back to cheaper effects under load." All `developmentonly defensive` ([dump 6821](https://github.com/SteamDatabase/GameTracking-Deadlock/blob/d251cf9d9a796061c90f58724230b7e17b97121b/DumpSource2/convars.txt)).
- The particle library logs "Warning: Missing fallback for particle system "%s"!", so systems without an authored fallback are not swapped ([particles strings](https://github.com/SteamDatabase/GameTracking-Deadlock/blob/d251cf9d9a796061c90f58724230b7e17b97121b/game/bin/win64/particles_strings.txt)). Particle definitions carry `m_hFallback` / `m_nFallbackMaxCount` (earlier note, CParticleSystemDefinition schema).
- alz cfg: `cl_particle_sim_fallback_threshold_ms "1"`, `cl_particle_sim_fallback_base_multiplier "100"`, `cl_particle_fallback_base "5"`, `cl_particle_fallback_multiplier "10"`.
- OptiLock 6.1: threshold `0`, base multiplier `2`, base `4`, multiplier `2` ([FPS Config](https://github.com/dacooderr/OptiLock/blob/8b78c0f9e09b767b60f7c90be473a9d3320b398e/OptiLock%20FPS%20Config%20(Recommended)/gameinfo.gi)).
- ghempy53 ran 2 ms / 20 / 2 / 5, then 1 ms / 100 / 5 / 10, with owner impressions only. Those Oct 6-7 impressions were later invalidated ([TESTED-CHANGES](https://github.com/ghempy53/deadlock-config/blob/7231a9e781584298b49560f01801cbfd4b52924f/docs/TESTED-CHANGES.md)).

### Inferences
- There are three defensible settings:
  - **Keep 1 / 100 / 5 / 10.** Most CPU relief in fights; effects look simpler in fights.
  - **Middle 2 / 20 / 2 / 5.** Fallbacks start later and ramp more gently.
  - **Valve 6 / 5 / 0 / 0.** Zero visual change except in extreme load, and fight CPU goes up.
- The fallback is an artist-made version of the same effect. Ability shape and timing should stay readable, so this is a fidelity cost, not an information cost. That separates it from `r_particle_max_detail_level`, `cl_particle_max_count` and `r_citadel_disable_particle_lights`.
- Recommendation: treat the current values as a deliberate, already-accepted trade, and don't count them as "free". If alz wants the strict rule, test the middle setting before going to Valve defaults, since going to defaults likely costs fight 1% lows.

### Gaps
- How many Deadlock effects actually ship fallbacks, and how different they look, is unknown (the compiled `.vpcf` files are not in GameTracking).
- No measurement of particle sim ms in fights. `cl_particle_sim_fallback_threshold_ms` could be calibrated with Valve's own perf overlay if one is reachable without `-dev`.

## Q5. Which threading settings fit a Ryzen 7 5700X?

### Takeaway
None need setting. The 5700X has one CCD and one core type, so `thread_pool_option` and `ThreadPoolCoreTypeRequest` (built for hybrid Intel CPUs) can't change scheduling. Every parallel switch already defaults to on. The only thread-count line in the cfg (`snd_steamaudio_num_threads 6`) should go back to Valve's 2. The remaining thread-level experiment is outside `gameinfo.gi`: SMT off in BIOS, which has player reports but no Deadlock-specific measurement on Zen 3.

### Cited Findings
- Engine strings: `Engine2/ThreadPoolCoreTypeRequest`, `thread_pool_option`, `GlobalThreadPoolMode`, `cfg/boot.vcfg`; tier0 logs "ProcessorCore %u - group %u, mask 0x%llx, flags 0x%x, efficiency %u" and ", efficiency class %lld" ([engine2](https://github.com/SteamDatabase/GameTracking-Deadlock/blob/d251cf9d9a796061c90f58724230b7e17b97121b/game/bin/win64/engine2_strings.txt); [tier0](https://github.com/SteamDatabase/GameTracking-Deadlock/blob/d251cf9d9a796061c90f58724230b7e17b97121b/game/bin/win64/tier0_strings.txt)). In CS2 the same convar is "CPU Cores Usage Preference" for P-core/E-core CPUs (earlier note Q3).
- Defaults already parallel ([dump 6821](https://github.com/SteamDatabase/GameTracking-Deadlock/blob/d251cf9d9a796061c90f58724230b7e17b97121b/DumpSource2/convars.txt)): `r_threaded_particles`, `r_threaded_particle_creation`, `r_threaded_scene_object_update`, `cl_parallel_readpacketentities` (threshold 2), `cl_pred_parallel_postnetwork`, `animgraph_parallel_postdataupdate`, `iv_parallel_latch`, `phys_step_threaded`, `soundsystem_update_async`, `snd_mix_async`, `r_texture_hookup_uses_threadpool` (all `true`). `citadel_camera_soft_collision_multi_thread 8` ("number of jobs to chunk the 128 traces"); `fs_async_threads -1` ("auto"); `sparseshadowtree_parallel_generation 2` (moot, since SST rendering is off in the cfg).
- No ConVar sets job-pool size. Earlier research rejected the `-threads N` launch option because it can only limit the pool (repo RESEARCH.md §5).
- Fight hitching scales with fight size even on a 9800X3D, with one core pinned. Process Lasso affinity doesn't help (secondhand forum digest, [ghempy53 FORUM-RESEARCH-2026-10-06](https://github.com/ghempy53/deadlock-config/blob/7231a9e781584298b49560f01801cbfd4b52924f/docs/research/FORUM-RESEARCH-2026-10-06.md)).

### Inferences
- On a single-CCD Zen 3 there is no cross-CCD latency to avoid, so core-affinity or CCD-pinning advice for 2-CCD Ryzens does not apply.
- The main thread is the bottleneck, so wider job pools can't help. Only less main-thread work (particles, voices, HUD) or less waiting (the job-sync lines, items 6-7 in Q1) can. That's why the Q1 candidates are scheduling switches rather than thread counts.
- With Reflex on, `engine_low_latency_sleep_after_client_tick` is the one "threading-adjacent" `release` switch worth a live test on this CPU.

### Gaps
- No Deadlock benchmark of SMT on vs off on Zen 3 (5700X) exists in the sources found. The reports are mostly Intel or unspecified.
- The mapping of `GlobalThreadPoolMode` in `boot.vcfg` is undocumented.
